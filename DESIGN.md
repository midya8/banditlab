# BanditLab method notes

This document explains the posterior state, Bellman recursion, rate-informed
upper bound and forward policy audit used by the code. All results are exact
under the supplied independent stationary Beta-Bernoulli model. They do not
check whether that model fits a real decision problem.

## Information state and Bellman induction

Arm i has one latent rate theta_i drawn from its independent Beta(a_i,b_i) prior. Conditional on all rates, arm rewards are independent Bernoulli draws with their arm's fixed rate. For an action/outcome history with s_i successes and f_i failures on each arm, the likelihood is proportional to the product of theta_i\*\*s_i\*(1-theta_i)\*\*f_i. Multiplying independent prior densities gives independent posterior Beta(a_i+s_i,b_i+f_i). History order adds no information once these counts are fixed. The posterior predictive probability of the next success is (a_i+s_i)/(a_i+b_i+s_i+f_i). Rates are not redrawn at every pull. Outcomes remain dependent marginally through the shared latent rate.

With h pulls remaining, condition on the selected arm's next reward. A success gives one reward and its updated continuation; failure gives zero reward and its other updated continuation. The weighted sum is Q(h,state,arm). V(0,state)=0; maximizing Q gives V(h,state). Induction over h proves that this is the optimal expected total reward among all non-anticipating history-dependent policies, including randomized ones: randomizing first actions cannot exceed the maximum of their expected values. Continuations use only observed data, never latent rates. Every maximizer is reported, and the first arm index is selected when there is a tie. No claim of unique policy or globally lexicographically first unmerged tree is made.

## Complete compressed graph and refusal contract

At elapsed depth d, distribute d success/failure increments among 2\*K categories. The number of nonnegative integer compositions is C(d+2\*K-1,2\*K-1). Every composition is reachable with positive prior predictive probability under some ordering of actions/outcomes because all starting shapes are positive. Summing d=0..H by the hockey-stick identity gives C(H+2\*K,2\*K) states. The initial priors are fixed, so distinct increment vectors give distinct posterior vectors at their depths. This is an exact state count for the full problem, not a heuristic upper estimate and not the exponential policy-tree count.

Generate compositions in lexicographic increment order, deepest layer first. Each action adds one success or failure increment, so every child already has an ID. Store all terminal states too. This returns complete alternative-action witnesses even for states unreachable under the selected policy. The number of edges is 2\*K times the number of nonterminal decision states. Preflight computes the full state count before generating states or expanding CDF polynomials. A refusal emits no partial result. The 1..3-arm, shape 1..16, H0..12 and state-limit 1..20000 bounds constrain the domain. Rational integer sizes, full JSON history size and execution costs are not implied by the state counter.

## Matched budgets, different feedback rules

At every posterior state, greedy chooses an arm with the largest current predictive mean, using first-index ties. Evaluate this entire adaptive policy recursively, including both posterior updates after every pull. The implementation also returns Q-values for forcing each first action and then following greedy; this is different from the optimized continuation Q-table. The baseline is not a single myopic reward compared with an H-step optimal total, nor a prior-mean arm frozen after data arrive. It has the same observations, unit rewards, priors and remaining pull budget as the optimum. Bellman optimality implies V_opt>=V_greedy at every state.

A fixed action sequence cannot react to outcomes. Its prior expected return is the sum of the selected arms' prior/posterior current means, with no observed update used in those fixed choices. Maximization over all fixed sequences therefore chooses a maximum-mean arm at every remaining step, yielding h\*max_mean. Thus the fixed-arm baseline is optimized over the full no-feedback sequence class, rather than chosen to be weak. It is distinct from the fully updating greedy policy.

## Clairvoyant expectation and Bayes regret

If a comparator knows theta before the first pull but cannot see future reward draws, it can choose a maximum-rate arm every round. Conditional expected reward is H\*max_i(theta_i). Every non-anticipating learning policy has conditional expected per-round reward at most max_i(theta_i), so integrating the prior gives an upper bound on its prior expected total reward. Subtracting each policy value gives Bayes regret under the supplied prior, not realized outcome-sequence regret or a pointwise frequentist guarantee. With one arm, this comparator adds no action-relevant information and its regret is exactly zero.

For independent rates, P(max(theta_i)<=x) is the product of their CDFs. Any random variable Z in [0,1] has E[Z]=integral_0^1 P(Z>x) dx. Therefore E[max theta]=1-integral_0^1 product(CDF_i) dx. For positive integer a,b, Beta density is x\*\*(a-1)\*(1-x)\*\*(b-1)/B(a,b), with reciprocal B(a,b)=(a+b-1)!/((a-1)!\*(b-1)!). Binomial expansion of (1-x)\*\*(b-1), followed by exact termwise integration, gives a finite rational CDF polynomial. Multiply these polynomials exactly, integrate each monomial c_j\*x\*\*j to c_j/(j+1), and subtract the sum from one. The returned polynomial includes zeros and uses increasing-power coefficient order. Alternating coefficients can be large; Fraction prevents cancellation rounding, not polynomial work or integer-size growth.

## Prescribed exploration witness

Take Beta(1,1) and Beta(5,4), whose means are 1/2 and 5/9. With one pull, arm 1 is strictly optimal. With two pulls both first-action values equal 10/9, so starting with arm 0 at that horizon is only a tie, not a strict exploration win. At three pulls, forcing arm 0 then optimizing gives 187/108; forcing arm 1 then optimizing gives 46/27. Greedy starts with arm 1 and its complete value is 46/27. Optimal gain is 1/36; the best fixed sequence is all arm 1 with value 5/3. After starting arm 0, success changes it to Beta(2,1) and the next selected arm is 0; failure changes it to Beta(1,2) and the next arm is 1. The full graph covers later choices as well.

For the latent upper bound, conditioning on theta 1 when theta 0 is uniform gives E[max(theta 0,theta 1)|theta 1]=(1+theta 1\*\*2)/2. The second moment of Beta(5,4) is 5\*6/(9\*10)=1/3. The integrated best rate is 2/3, so the three-pull bound is 2 and optimal Bayes regret is 29/108. The six stated horizons are a declared educational fixture. They are not a held-out benchmark or evidence about arbitrary priors, Thompson sampling, UCB or real applications.

## Independent verification

The policy oracle builds every complete binary observation tree, enumerates all reward bit strings and follows actions through the tree. For each arm it integrates theta\*\*s\*(1-theta)\*\*f directly by the Beta ratio B(a+s,b+f)/B(a,b), computed from factorials. Multiplying arm moments yields each outcome path's marginal probability. Sum path probability times total unit rewards. No production compiler, posterior recurrence, state generator or Bellman backup is used. All 128 depth-three two-arm trees for each of 16 prior pairs give 2048 references; all 27 depth-two three-arm trees for each of 64 prior triples give 1728. Grouping tree values by first action checks every root Q-value and all first-action ties. Independently generated greedy trees check that baseline's full value.

A different graph audit reconstructs all 462 states for three arms and horizon 5, every posterior update, probability, child ID, Q-value, greedy continuation and tie. Boundary checks execute the 1820-state two-arm H12 and 3003-state three-arm H8 cases. Mocked guards ensure state-budget refusal happens before state generation or clairvoyant integration. Zero horizon, single-arm/no-information identities, prior persistence, ties, strict types, CLI errors and input/output/RNG isolation cover the contract.

For all 256 starting shape pairs, a separate CDF reference uses the binomial tail sum from j=a to a+b-1 of C(a+b-1,j)\*x\*\*j\*(1-x)\*\*(a+b-1-j) at stated rational points. Its integral-derived first moment must equal a/(a+b). To verify the maximum-rate integral without the production monomial multiplication, enumerate products of these Bernstein terms and integrate each x\*\*s\*(1-x)\*\*f using factorial Beta moments. Checks include one/two/three uniform arms, asymmetric priors, the demo, and three Beta(16,16) arms. This is exact finite-polynomial verification, not quadrature or random simulation.

The SVG embeds the entire unchanged demo, including all state graphs and polynomial coefficients, as zlib-compressed base64 in its metadata element (`data-encoding="zlib+base64"`). Decode with the Python standard library: `json.loads(zlib.decompress(base64.b64decode(metadata_text)))`. Compression changes storage only, not plotted values or the JSON equality check. Four tests verify all 28 plotted vertices against exact rational values under a common coordinate transform, full compressed metadata, byte regeneration and rejection of a changed undisplayed terminal state or CDF coefficient. Visual inspection checks the actual pixels in addition to these geometric tests.

## Forward occupancy and performance difference

Write pi(a|s) for a complete randomized action kernel, P(r,s'|s,a) for the posterior predictive reward/next-state probability, and d_t(s) for its occupancy at elapsed pull t. Start with root mass one. For every action/outcome, send d_t(s) pi(a|s) P(r,s'|s,a) to the child. Sum all incoming flows, including distinct histories merged into the same sufficient-count state. Probability induction gives unit total mass at every depth and at the terminal layer. Summing reward times edge flow gives the prior expected total reward. This is not a simulation, count of paths or probability assigned to the unknown latent-rate vector.

Independently evaluate V_pi(s) backward by averaging reward plus V_pi(child) under pi and P. Check that its root value agrees exactly with the forward reward sum. All zero-mass states keep their conditional policy values and complete kernels, but have zero reward/loss contribution. A custom kernel must cover all decision states, not only those it visits; terminal rows are empty. This supports information-state Markov randomization with independent conditional draws, not a user-supplied policy with hidden history or correlated private randomness. The solver's optimum already dominates such randomization because each weighted first-action value cannot exceed its maximum.

Define g(s) = V_star(s) - sum_a pi(a|s) Q_star(s,a). Bellman optimality makes every g(s) nonnegative. Substituting Q_star as expected immediate reward plus next V_star gives expected g at depth t as expected V_star at depth t minus expected reward at depth t minus expected V_star at depth t+1. Sum over the finite horizon. Every interior V_star term cancels, terminal V_star is zero, and root occupancy is one. Thus sum_s d(s) g(s) = V_star(root) - V_pi(root). This is a policy performance-difference identity under the stated prior, not a causal attribution or frequentist regret guarantee. The gap must use optimal-continuation Q, not the current policy's continuation Q. The latter would give zero on-policy Bellman residual even for a bad policy. Subtracting the same policy value from the clairvoyant bound also gives Bayes regret = optimal Bayes regret + this loss.

The three-pull fixture's optimal/greedy/fixed positive-mass counts are 14/14/10 out of the full 35-state graph. Greedy loses 1/36 at the first decision and zero at later occupied decisions. Fixed loses 1/36 + 8/297 + 1/99 = 7/108. Expected arm-0 pulls are 11/6, 2/3 and zero. These numbers are consequences of this specified model and each policy's own occupancy; they do not establish an empirical causal mechanism or universal exploration ranking.

Eleven new tests check 144 named-policy cases (16 prior pairs, horizons 1/2/3, three policies). It does not use production edges or posterior predictive transitions. Its optimal policy choices come from exhaustive complete policy-tree integration rather than the production Bellman recursion. Edge tests cover zero horizon, a single arm, alternate optimal ties, zero-mass conditional states, strict kernel validation including unreachable rows, CLI, preflight and isolation. ## Sources and implementation scope

Min, Maglaras and Moallemi, Thompson Sampling with Information Relaxation Penalties, Sections 2 and 3, [paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.04251v1), describes the standard Bayesian information-state objective, conjugate Bernoulli example and parameter-informed bound. BanditLab solves a deliberately small full finite-state problem with its own code and independent oracles. It does not implement IRS, Thompson sampling, Gittins indices, asymptotic regret results or general large-scale methods. No external code/data is copied. MIT licensed. Independent stationary latent rates and correctly specified priors are assumptions, not facts established by exact arithmetic.
